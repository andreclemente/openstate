# Evidence Guide

OpenState requires evidence for every issue. This guide helps you find, evaluate, and present evidence effectively.

---

## What counts as evidence?

Evidence is **verifiable information** that supports the existence or impact of a problem. It must be factual, not opinion-based.

### Accepted evidence types

| Type | Description | Where to find it |
|:-----|:-------------|:-----------------|
| **News articles** | Investigative journalism, reports from credible media | Newspaper archives, media websites |
| **Official data** | Government statistics, public reports | Transparency portals, open data platforms |
| **Legislation** | Laws, regulations, decrees, official directives | Official gazettes, government websites |
| **Real cases** | Factual descriptions of concrete situations | Ombudsman reports, court decisions |
| **Studies** | Academic research, NGO reports, audits | University repositories, organization websites |

---

## How to find good evidence

### 1. Start with official sources

Government transparency portals and open data platforms are the most reliable sources:

- **Transparency portals** — Look for performance metrics, audit reports, and public spending data
- **Open data catalogs** — Download raw datasets to support quantitative claims
- **Official reports** — Regulatory bodies, auditors, and inspectors publish periodic assessments

### 2. Cross-reference with journalism

Investigative journalism often uncovers problems that official data alone doesn't reveal:

- Search newspaper archives for specific public services or institutions
- Look for patterns across multiple news reports
- Prefer primary sources (original documents, data) over secondary analysis

### 3. Use legislation and regulation

Legal documents establish what *should* happen, making it easier to show the gap between policy and reality:

- Cite specific articles or clauses when a public service fails to meet legal requirements
- Compare implementation timelines against legal deadlines
- Reference court decisions that have found systemic failures

### 4. Document real cases

Concrete examples make abstract problems tangible:

- Include dates, locations, and specific outcomes
- Reference official case numbers when available (ombudsman complaints, court cases)
- Describe the actual experience, not just the general pattern

## How to present evidence in an issue

### Structure

List each piece of evidence with:

1. **What** — A brief description of the evidence
2. **Source** — Where it came from (with URL when possible)
3. **Relevance** — How it supports the problem claim

### Example

```
## Evidence

- Hospital triage wait times exceeded 4 hours in 60% of cases
  Source: Health Regulatory Authority Annual Report 2024, p. 45
  Relevance: Demonstrates systematic failure in emergency prioritization

- News investigation found patients leaving without being seen
  Source: Público, "Hospital emergencies under pressure", 15/03/2024
  Relevance: Corroborates data with real-world observations

- Decree-Law 123/2020 mandates maximum 1-hour triage wait
  Source: Diário da República, 1st series, no. 234/2020
  Relevance: Shows legal requirement is not being met
```

## Evidence quality checklist

Before submitting, verify:

- [ ] Each piece of evidence has a **source** (URL, document reference, or publication)
- [ ] Evidence is **verifiable** — others can check it independently
- [ ] Evidence is **relevant** — it directly supports the problem claim
- [ ] Evidence is **recent** — within the last 2-3 years unless historical context is needed
- [ ] Evidence is **specific** — concrete numbers, dates, and facts rather than generalizations

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's a problem | What to do instead |
|:--------|:-------------------|:-------------------|
| Linking to social media posts | Not verifiable, can be deleted | Find the original source |
| Citing personal experience only | Not reproducible on its own | Support with official data or news |
| Using outdated data | Problem may have been resolved | Check for the most recent reports |
| Vague references ("everyone knows") | Not evidence | Find specific, citable sources |
| Opinion pieces | Not factual evidence | Use data, reports, or investigative journalism |
